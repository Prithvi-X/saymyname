01 / DESIGN SYSTEM

Purpose, authority and relationship to the nine product documents

This document defines the visual identity and digital design language for SayMyName. It is not a mood board and it is not permission for an agent to improvise a new aesthetic on every screen.

APPROVED DIRECTION

Primary environment: dark cinematic. Typography: contemporary neo-grotesk led by Instrument Sans. Art direction: a new synthesis of sculptural objects, architectural geometry and selective digital fragmentation. The unifying concept is THE INTERVAL.

What this document controls

Brand premise, wordmark behaviour and symbolic direction.

Colour, typography, composition, geometry, surfaces and imagery.

Graphic devices, 3D art direction, motion language and interaction character.

How these principles translate into web and mobile screens.

Rules for designers and Antigravity when creating or changing visual components.

Relationship to Document 03

Document 10 becomes the source of truth for brand identity and visual style. It supersedes the earlier Warm Editorial Minimalism direction where that direction conflicts with this document. In particular, the previous warm-ivory canvas, oxblood-led palette, serif-led page headings, pill-shaped navigation and centred narrow-column composition are no longer the approved visual direction.

Document 03 remains authoritative for information architecture, screen inventory, responsive behaviour, interaction requirements and accessibility requirements unless a visual decision directly conflicts with Document 10. Documents 01, 02, 04, 05, 06, 07, 08 and 09 remain in force for product scope, flows, features, engineering, data, testing, delivery and agent operation.

What this document does not authorise

Changing authentication, student eligibility, mutual-consent or privacy behaviour for aesthetic reasons.

Introducing new product features or changing API/database contracts.

Copying any reference website, television franchise, logo, proprietary type treatment or signature artwork.

Treating untested palettes, fonts, motion timings or layouts as proven user preferences.

IMPLEMENTATION RULE

When style and function conflict, preserve the approved product behaviour and access rules. Rework the visual treatment around the requirement; do not weaken the requirement.

The visual language is built around the space between two separate identities. The interval is not emptiness for decoration. It is the moment before recognition.

Concept diagram only. This is not final logo artwork. It illustrates two distinct masses, a deliberate negative space and a third visual relationship that appears through alignment.

06 / DESIGN SYSTEM

Wordmark, symbol and the subtle name reference

The product name already carries the strongest brand idea. The visual system should give that name authority rather than adding a competing mascot or decorative emblem.

Wordmark direction

Use the exact brand spelling: SayMyName. Preserve the internal capital letters.

Move away from the current serif wordmark. Explore a confident modern grotesk with a custom optical correction.

Investigate a bespoke treatment of the N or M where a shared cut or negative space becomes a signature detail.

Do not add routine labels such as PHASE 1, VERIFIED or IITM BS as part of the logo lockup.

Prepare horizontal, compact and symbol-only versions only after the primary wordmark is approved.

Symbol direction: the interval mark

Explore a mark made from two distinct solid forms that face one another across a deliberate gap. When aligned, the gap creates a third shared shape. The mark should be recognisable at small sizes in a single flat colour. It must not depend on glow, gradients, transparency or animated effects to be understood.

Subtle Breaking Bad inspiration

The reference is the conceptual force of the phrase "Say my name": identity, recognition, confidence and being known. Translate that into the weight of the wordmark and the moment two forms align. Do not reproduce the series title typography, periodic-table title treatment, character imagery, costumes, hazmat motifs, chemical labels or any franchise logo.

IP AND BRAND INDEPENDENCE

The relationship is conceptual only. The SayMyName identity must be original, independently recognisable and usable without any viewer knowing the television reference.

No purple-to-blue, purple-to-orange or multi-stop mesh gradients.

Dark environment is not a licence for low contrast

Dark mode can be preferred in some environments, but it is not universally more legible or more comfortable. Phase 1 uses the dark cinematic environment as its approved default. The exact hues are initial prototype tokens and must be validated on real screens and displays before final lock. Keep colour roles semantic so an intentionally designed light appearance can be developed later if user research justifies it. Do not add an unvalidated theme toggle now.

09 / DESIGN SYSTEM

Typography: contemporary neo-grotesk

Typography should feel modern, self-assured and human. The interface should not depend on editorial serifs, condensed techno fonts or excessive monospaced labels.

Primary typeface decision

INSTRUMENT SANS

Use Instrument Sans as the primary product family for the first design prototype. It is a variable neo-grotesk with weight and width axes and stylistic alternates. Its precision and subtle warmth fit the desired direction better than a generic geometric SaaS font or a serif-led editorial system.

License: Instrument Sans is released under the SIL Open Font License 1.1. Include its license file and attribution notices in the repository when self-hosting or bundling. Source and current license must be checked at implementation time.

Focus, validation and state boundaries

Keyboard focus must remain clearly visible on every interactive control. Use a consistent high-contrast focus treatment with a small offset; never remove the browser focus indicator without an accessible replacement.

Hover, focus, pressed, selected, disabled, loading and error states must be visually distinct. Do not rely on colour alone to communicate state.

Form labels remain visible above their fields. Place helper text and validation messages consistently below the control, and associate them programmatically.

A disabled control must still communicate why it is unavailable when that reason is important to task completion.

13 / DESIGN SYSTEM

Photography and human imagery

The product is about real people. Human photography must never look like synthetic stock imagery or an artificial beauty campaign.

Profile photography

Use actual user-uploaded images for real profiles, subject to the product's moderation and consent requirements.

Never present AI-generated portraits, stock portraits or internal demo images as real verified members.

When using test fixtures, visibly label the profiles as synthetic demo data and prevent them from entering production.

Preserve natural skin tone and realistic lighting. Do not apply blue/purple colour casts to every portrait.

Do not force all portraits into the same crop, pose or studio background.

Provide clear image crop, reorder, remove and preview behaviour to the user.

Campaign imagery may be more cinematic than member photography. Use a controlled light source, deliberate shadows, tactile materials, carefully composed environments and authentic human expression. Avoid over-retouching, fake HDR, extreme teal-orange grading and synthetic neon atmospheres.

Lighting and rendering rules

One clear key light and one controlled edge or fill light are usually sufficient.

Use real shadow and material response; do not add bloom to compensate for weak form.

Easing

Use an ease-out curve for elements entering or responding, and a restrained ease-in for exit. Initial implementation candidates: cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.7, 0.2, 1) for entering; cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 1, 1) for exiting. Measure the result on real devices. Avoid overshoot, elastic bounce and long cinematic delays in routine workflows.

Signature interaction: mutual match

1. Two distinct forms are shown with a clear separation.

2. They move towards a shared alignment, with the gap becoming the central visual event.

3. The shared form resolves briefly and clearly, communicating mutuality.

4. The user receives a simple next action: start a conversation or continue discovery.

No confetti, coins, fireworks, celebratory counters or forced conversation prompt. The animation must not obscure the person's name or the next action.

Motion restrictions

Respect prefers-reduced-motion and provide a static equivalent.

Do not animate large background shapes continuously.

Do not use parallax as a default.

Do not animate layout dimensions when transform or opacity will work.

Do not use transitions to hide slow loading or delayed API responses.

Do not use sound or haptics without a clear user benefit and user control.

16 / DESIGN SYSTEM

Screen translation I: public, onboarding and discovery

These directions translate the design language into key product surfaces without changing the approved product flows.

Public homepage

Use an asymmetric 12-column composition. Place the primary headline and product explanation on one side and one original sculptural/convergence visual on the other.

The headline should be left-aligned and confidently sized. Do not vertically centre every element in a narrow column.

The public navigation should be simple: brand, Eligibility, Safety & Consent, Sign In and Register.

The visual should support the product promise, not compete with registration or eligibility actions.

No fake member portraits, fake activity, fake match counts or unsupported legal/security claims.

Registration and verification

Use a purposeful two-region desktop layout only if it improves understanding: concise context on one side, form on the other.

Keep the form aligned, labels above inputs and field widths consistent.

Show the verification stages as a clear progress sequence, not a row of pill-shaped capsules.

Never prefill sample student data or passwords.

Keep eligibility, adult verification and current student verification visibly distinct.

Discover

At desktop width, use a substantial profile region with enough room for the person and their story, plus a contextual rail only where it adds useful information.

Avoid a narrow single-column card floating in a wide empty screen.

Profile name, programme, location where allowed, bio, interests and prompts must have clear hierarchy.

Actions should be immediately understandable. Like and Pass must work with pointer, keyboard and touch.

The discovery interface should support considered choice, not infinite consumption or fake scarcity.

17 / DESIGN SYSTEM

Screen translation II: Likes, Matches, Chats and Profile

Core interaction screens should feel quieter than campaign art. The brand should remain present through typography, spacing, shape and state feedback.

Likes and Matches

Use a deliberate list or large-row structure. Do not default to small floating cards with excessive whitespace.

Make Like Back, Pass, Message and Unmatch labels explicit and consistent with the mutual-consent rules.

A received Like must never imply permission to message.

Design zero, one and many states. Do not fabricate profiles to fill the screen.

Chats

Use a stable two-pane desktop layout: a useful conversation list and a broad message thread.

The conversation thread should prioritise legible message width and stable vertical rhythm.

The composer remains visible and functional as the viewport changes or the mobile keyboard appears.

Organise call, Together Space, Relationship Mode and Safety actions. Do not show a row of unexplained icons.

Use honest privacy copy only. Do not expose internal document references or claim unimplemented encryption.

Loading, empty, failed, offline and reconnecting states are part of the design, not developer leftovers.

Profile and settings

Separate the public profile editor from confidential verification and account information.

Never display institutional email, roll number, date of birth or verification documents on the public profile.

Use structured sections rather than one endless form.

Use real upload and crop interactions for profile photos, not a raw URL field for ordinary users.

Saving, validation errors and unsaved changes must be obvious.

18 / DESIGN SYSTEM

Screen translation III: safety, consent and Relationship Mode

The brand must never make a sensitive or consequential decision feel like a playful trick.

Safety and consent

Use calm, clear language and a visible information hierarchy.

Report, Block, Unmatch and consent actions must remain discoverable and unambiguous.

Use high-contrast focus and destructive states. Do not hide safety actions behind decorative gesture-only interactions.

A status indicator should explain what it means, not rely only on colour or an icon.

Relationship Mode

Create a distinct atmosphere through composition and shared-object imagery, not by turning it into a dashboard of partner activity.

The shared space should feel like a place the two people choose to enter together.

Use the convergence language sparingly: shared calendars, memories and activities can use paired visual elements, but not constant split-screen motifs.

Mutual activation must be explicit, and either partner must be able to leave independently.

Never introduce read monitoring, GPS tracking, partner activity scoring or any other surveillance pattern.

Audio calls and virtual dates

Calls should use an uncluttered, high-clarity environment with immediate controls and clear call state. Virtual dates can use more expressive sculptural and spatial art direction, but functionality, network quality and accessibility remain primary. The visual treatment must not obstruct mute, speaker, leave or safety actions.

19 / DESIGN SYSTEM

Accessibility, responsiveness and performance

Cinematic does not mean inaccessible. The dark environment must remain readable on inexpensive displays, under brightness changes and with accessibility settings enabled.

Accessibility baseline

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for web interfaces.

Normal text contrast must be at least 4.5:1; large text at least 3:1. Non-text controls and focus indicators must meet the relevant non-text contrast requirements.

Do not use thin weights for small text. Provide visible keyboard focus.

Support text resize and reflow without loss of content or functionality.

Respect reduced-motion settings. WCAG 2.2 includes an AAA criterion allowing interaction-triggered motion to be disabled; treat that as a design goal.

Provide keyboard operation and clear accessible names for icon controls.

Do not make dragging, swiping or hover the only way to perform an action.

Visual review procedure

1. Capture the same screens at 1920x1080, 1440x900, 1280x800, 768px, 430px, 390px and 360px.

2. Review public, unverified and verified states separately. Do not use private member data in visual fixtures.

3. Review empty, loading, error, selected, disabled, hover, focus and success states.

4. Check typography, contrast, alignment, scroll behaviour, image crop and content density at 100% zoom.

5. Run keyboard and reduced-motion checks. Record actual results, not impressions.
